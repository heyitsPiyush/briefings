AI × Cybersecurity

deep

The intersection of AI and cybersecurity saw significant developments in September 2026, with AI increasingly serving as both an offensive and defensive tool. Threat actors are utilizing autonomous AI agents in sophisticated campaigns, such as those reported by Gambit targeting online retailers, leveraging open-source tools like Strix and Cairn for vulnerability research and exploitation against models such as GLM 5.2 and DeepSeek v4 Pro. Google's Threat Intelligence Group also documented attackers moving towards agentic workflows, using generative AI for reconnaissance, malware development, and credential harvesting, with one incident compromising a cloud resource and executing a mass credential-harvesting campaign in under six hours. Microsoft disrupted EvilTokens, an AI-powered cybercrime platform that facilitated financial fraud by analyzing inboxes and drafting deceptive messages, demonstrating AI's role in scaling online abuse. Additionally, Huntress observed the abuse of ChatGPT Custom GPTs to deliver remote access trojans (RATs) via ClickFix lures.

Regarding LLM vulnerabilities and securing AI systems, prompt injection remains a top concern, ranked as LLM01 by the OWASP Foundation. OpenAI's internal red-teaming system, GPT-Red, discovered a new category of self-replicating prompt injection attack, akin to a computer worm, on models like GPT-5.4-mini, although these were contained within simulated environments. Anthropic's red-teaming efforts highlighted weak safeguards in Zhipu AI's GLM-5.3, demonstrating its potential for malicious content generation and cyberattacks, with guardrails bypassable through deceptive prompts or prefilling thinking tokens. Research on securing Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems emphasizes that RAG introduces a new attack surface, requiring robust security across the entire data pipeline, including protections against corpus poisoning, hidden document injection, and sensitive-data leakage, as outlined by HexTyx and OWASP. Defensive AI tools are also advancing, with GTT Communications launching GTT Defense Halo, an AI-powered platform for proactive threat hunting and automated remediation of vulnerabilities. OpenAI also expanded Daybreak, its program offering advanced cyber capabilities to trusted defenders, with tiers like Daybreak Blue and Daybreak Red providing access to models such as GPT-5.6 Sol and GPT-5.6-Cyber for vulnerability discovery and exploit validation.
