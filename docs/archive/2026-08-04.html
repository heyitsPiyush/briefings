Bug Bounty

deep

Recent bug bounty news highlights a significant shift in the industry, largely driven by the increasing use of artificial intelligence in vulnerability discovery. Apple, for instance, implemented caps and a 30-day cool-off period on bug bounty submissions starting in June 2026 due to a surge in “AI slop” reports—low-quality, AI-generated findings that overwhelmed their review teams. This led to legitimate submissions, like a macOS privilege-escalation exploit chain discovered by Italian startup Bynario, initially being blocked before Apple directly engaged with the researchers. Similarly, GitHub restructured its bug bounty program into a two-tier system from July 27, 2026, reducing public payout ranges and creating a higher-paying, invite-only VIP tier, explicitly citing the need to curb AI-generated reports and prioritize submissions from verified security researchers. Coinbase also revised its HackerOne payout structure on July 29, 2026, slashing critical vulnerability rewards by 70%, from $50,000 to $15,000, attributing this to AI models mass-producing vulnerability reports.

Microsoft announced paying over $20 million through its bug bounty programs between July 1, 2025, and June 30, 2026, to 562 researchers across 64 countries, with the largest single payout reaching $200,000. This period saw 2,531 eligible reports, noting a significant increase in submission volume in the latter half of the year, partly due to AI-supported security research. In other updates, HackerOne will require government-issued ID verification for all paid bug bounty submissions starting August 14, 2026, to meet regulatory requirements. July 2026 also saw significant vulnerability disclosures, including a critical authentication bypass in Microsoft SharePoint, CVE-2026-55040, with a CVSS score of 9.1, discovered by Rapid7 Senior Principal Security Researcher Stephen Fewer. Additionally, a Windows BitLocker security feature bypass, CVE-2026-50661, with a CVSS score of 6.1, was publicly disclosed, requiring physical access to exploit.

Hardware Hacking

deep

Hardware hacking and embedded security news as of August 2026 highlights significant firmware vulnerabilities and escalating IoT threats. A critical firmware flaw, CVE-2025-41711, allows unauthenticated remote attackers to extract password hashes from industrial control system firmware images, enabling brute force attacks due to weak cryptographic algorithms (CWE-327). This impacts Janitza and Weidmüller industrial devices. Another notable incident involves a firmware bug in Coldcard hardware wallets, exploited to drain approximately $89 million in Bitcoin from thousands of addresses since July 30, 2026. This vulnerability stemmed from weak random-number generation in older firmware, affecting wallet seed phrase reconstruction. Additionally, a command injection flaw, CVE-2026-16812, in Arista VeloCloud Orchestrator (VCO) on-premises versions, rated with a CVSS score of 10.0, is under active exploitation, potentially leading to arbitrary code execution and compromise of confidentiality, integrity, and availability.

The IoT landscape continues to face pervasive threats, with botnet activity spiking sharply; for instance, a Mirai clone upgrade on September 2, 2025, led to attacks from 1,169 different IP addresses. Network equipment, specifically routers and switches, now average 32 vulnerabilities per device, with 34% of these being critical in enterprise networks. Furthermore, injection-class vulnerabilities have seen a dramatic resurgence, with generic injection (CWE-74) growing by 746% and SQL injection increasing by 75% in 2025, affecting various devices and systems. AI is also increasingly used in adversary operations, accelerating attack speed and targeting AI infrastructure and software supply chains, as seen with China-nexus adversaries exploiting critical vulnerabilities within 24 hours of public proof-of-concept release.
