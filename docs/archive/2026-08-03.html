Hardware Hacking

deep

Hardware hacking and embedded security news this August highlights a significant firmware vulnerability in Coldcard hardware wallets, leading to approximately $89 million in Bitcoin theft across 4,585 addresses. The flaw, active since a March 2021 firmware integration error, rerouted seed generation to a deterministic software pseudorandom number generator (PRNG) instead of the intended STM32 hardware random number generator, allowing attackers to reconstruct private keys offline. Coinkite has released emergency firmware updates, but users with previously generated seeds are advised to create new ones and transfer funds. Additionally, the FBI and EPA issued a joint advisory on July 30 regarding hackers targeting internet-connected Rockwell Automation/Allen-Bradley MicroLogix 1100 and 1400 PLCs at water utilities, leading to operational disruptions like pressure loss and flooding.

In other developments, side-channel attacks, particularly power analysis techniques (SPA, DPA, CPA), are increasingly being mitigated through firmware and software updates, recognizing the financial impracticality of hardware re-spins for legacy and IoT devices. Research is also exploring fault injection attacks on AI-enabled hardware, specifically edge AI systems running on neural processing units (NPUs), while simultaneously investigating how AI, including large language models, can assist in fault injection testing methodologies. The overall IoT security market is experiencing rapid growth, driven by the demand for advanced cybersecurity, AI-powered protection, and secure IoT ecosystems against evolving threats like ransomware and botnet attacks.
