Bug Bounty

deep

The bug bounty landscape witnessed significant activity in late August and early September 2026, with major vulnerability disclosures across prominent platforms. Microsoft's September Patch Tuesday addressed a record-breaking 972 vulnerabilities, including two actively exploited zero-days and 113 critical flaws. Notably, a critical remote code execution vulnerability (CVE-2026-73009) in Windows Secure Socket Tunneling Protocol (SSTP) with a CVSS score of 9.8 was patched. Additionally, a new Microsoft Defender zero-day exploit, "ShieldCrash," was publicly disclosed by researcher Nightmare Eclipse, reportedly bypassing a previously patched privilege escalation flaw. Google also fixed a Chrome V8 zero-day (CVE-2026-87491), an out-of-bounds write bug reported by Jihyeon Jeong, earning a $2,500 bounty.

Program updates and payouts also saw substantial movement. HackerOne announced the integration of OpenAI GPT Cyber Models to accelerate security finding fixes and introduced mandatory identity verification for bug bounty program submissions in August. Intigriti became the new provider for Adobe's Bug Bounty Program starting September 1, 2026, and introduced "CrowdRecon" to turn hacker reconnaissance into security intelligence. HackenProof reported over $500,000 in August payouts, with top awards including $50,000 to moamenmahmodd, $30,000 to BoatmanKharon, and $25,000 to Sagamore. The Web3 space continued to offer substantial rewards, with Usual's protocol bounty on Sherlock standing as the largest in tech history at $16 million.

Hardware Hacking

deep

In recent hardware hacking news, a critical firmware vulnerability (CVE-2026-33197) has been identified in AMI's Aptio V UEFI firmware, impacting numerous devices from vendors like Gigabyte and Cisco UCS servers, potentially allowing arbitrary code execution and bypassing Secure Boot. Furthermore, Cisco IOS XR routers were compromised by a state-sponsored actor known as "Fire Ant," who established covert access via a persistent GRE tunnel to intercept and redirect traffic, effectively transforming the routers into surveillance platforms. GeoVision also addressed 23 flaws in its license plate recognition cameras, which could enable authentication bypass and administrative account hijacking. These incidents underscore the persistent threat of firmware-level attacks across various embedded systems.

Significant developments in embedded security research include new fault-based attacks against Ascon, the lightweight cipher standardized by NIST in 2025 for IoT protection. Researchers demonstrated practical voltage fault injection attacks on post-quantum cryptographic signature schemes like Dilithium, XMSS, and SPHINCS+, successfully forging signatures on real microcontroller hardware by targeting verification logic. In RF hacking, Great Scott Gadgets unveiled the HackRF Pro, an upgraded Software Defined Radio platform offering enhanced performance and flexibility for wireless development and signal analysis across a broad frequency range. The growing IoT attack surface is further highlighted by a 107% surge in IoT attacks in H1 2024, with 98% of IoT device traffic remaining unencrypted.
