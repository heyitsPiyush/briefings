deep

The embedded security landscape in late 2026 reveals significant activity across multiple attack vectors. Researchers from Graz University of Technology uncovered "decades-old" side-channel vulnerabilities in file-notification subsystems affecting major operating systems including Linux, Windows, macOS, and Android, potentially allowing unprivileged access to infer keystrokes, visited websites, or steal credentials. Another notable side-channel attack, FROST (fingerprinting remotely using OPFS-based SSD timing), demonstrates how malicious websites can infer user activity on a computer by measuring minute delays in SSD operations through browser storage features. Firmware remains a critical target, with a D-Link DAP-1360 router vulnerability (CVE-2026-95675) allowing unauthenticated remote code execution as root, enabling full device compromise. Similarly, potential vulnerabilities in MikroTik RouterOS software have prompted Russia's telecommunications regulator to advise operators to inspect and update equipment to prevent authentication bypass and full device control.

Supply chain security continues to be a major concern, highlighted by the ChainDrop worm which poisoned 444 npm packages in August 2026, impacting billions of monthly downloads. This emphasizes the shift in trust boundaries from networks to build pipelines, where a single compromised build can affect numerous downstream consumers. New hardware hacking tools mentioned for 2026 include the Flipper Zero, Rubber Ducky, WiFi Pineapple, Proxmark3, and HackRF, indicating ongoing advancements in accessible penetration testing hardware. Furthermore, NIST published a report in September 2026 on strengthening hardware security across the semiconductor ecosystem, focusing on unified standards, traceability, and verifiable components throughout the supply chain. The EU Cyber Resilience Act, with its first enforcement milestone in September 2026, is driving silicon and platform vendors to integrate hardware roots of trust, secure boot, and continuous vulnerability handling into their security architectures.
