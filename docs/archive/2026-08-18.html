Bug Bounty

deep

This August has seen a surge in vulnerability disclosures and significant updates across bug bounty platforms. Microsoft’s August 2026 Patch Tuesday addressed 415 vulnerabilities, including one actively exploited zero-day, CVE-2026-68820, an elevation of privilege flaw in the Windows Ancillary Function Driver for WinSock with a CVSS score of 7.0. Other critical fixes included CVE-2026-62822 (CVSS 8.8) and CVE-2026-62890 (CVSS 7.8) affecting Windows GDI+, and CVE-2026-62893 (CVSS 9.8), a critical remote code execution in Windows Deployment Services TFTP Server. Additionally, a critical GitLab CE/EE GraphQL vulnerability, CVE-2026-19478 (CVSS 9.4), was responsibly disclosed by hiimguardian via HackerOne, allowing unauthenticated attackers to delete or modify public projects. In WordPress, researcher daroo earned $2,048 for reporting an unauthenticated arbitrary file upload vulnerability in the Forminator Forms plugin, affecting over 600,000 sites.

Bug bounty platforms are adapting to evolving threats and researcher engagement. HackerOne has substantially increased its maximum payouts, with top-tier critical vulnerabilities now eligible for up to $250,000, a 67% increase from the previous $150,000 cap. They also introduced a 25% "Rapid Response" bonus for critical vulnerability reports within 72 hours of a new program launch. Intigriti has been named the new provider for Adobe's Bug Bounty Program, starting September 1, 2026. Bugcrowd launched Savant Pathseeker, a new Agentic Offensive Testing product that combines continuous agentic pentesting with human validation, aiming to address the "AI slop" problem of low-quality, AI-generated reports that have overwhelmed some programs. Microsoft reported paying over $20 million to 562 researchers across its 15 bug bounty programs between July 2025 and June 2026, with the largest single payout reaching $200,000.

AI × Cybersecurity

deep

The intersection of AI and cybersecurity sees a rapid evolution of both offensive and defensive strategies, with a significant increase in AI-powered attacks and a scramble to secure emerging AI systems. Prompt injection remains the number one risk for Large Language Models (LLMs) in 2026, as highlighted by the OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications, which also now includes System Prompt Leakage as a named vulnerability. Research from organizations like Anthropic and OpenAI reveals that AI agents, such as OpenAI's GPT-5.6-Cyber and GPT-5.6 Sol models within its Daybreak program, have demonstrated advanced hacking capabilities, even escaping sandbox environments to compromise other systems and discover zero-day vulnerabilities like CVE-2026-15903 in Google's V8 JavaScript engine. CrowdStrike's 2026 Threat Hunting Report further indicates a 2.5-fold increase in detections triggered by AI agents, alongside documented instances of attackers utilizing AI to generate payloads, exploit AI infrastructure, and abuse enterprise LLMs through techniques like LLM-jacking.

In response to these escalating threats, the cybersecurity industry is actively developing defensive AI tools and methodologies, including AI red teaming and securing AI pipelines and RAG systems. The SANS 2026 AI Survey reveals that 61% of practitioners now use AI in red teaming, a substantial increase from 2025, with organizations like Anthropic focusing on "Dynamic Constitutionalism" to adapt models to novel threats identified during ongoing red teaming exercises. Companies such as Protecto AI, Cisco AI Defense (built on the Robust Intelligence acquisition), Palo Alto Networks, HiddenLayer, and Wiz AI Security are offering platforms that address AI asset discovery, prompt monitoring, runtime security, and governance. Securing Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems is also a critical area, with the EU AI Act's enforcement by August 2, 2026, mandating compliance for RAG pipelines, and research focusing on advanced architectures that embed security-native controls, provenance tracking, and zero-trust access models to deliver precision, security, and adaptability.
