deep

As of October 12, 2026, the embedded security landscape is marked by significant firmware vulnerabilities and escalating supply chain attacks. Samsung released its October 2026 security update, addressing nine critical Android vulnerabilities and several Samsung-specific flaws that could allow local attackers to execute arbitrary code, including use-after-free in its WSM service and out-of-bounds writes in the text-to-speech library. UEFI firmware implementations, specifically Insyde H2O BIOS, are also vulnerable to remote code execution due to a lack of verified boot enforcement against certain Firmware Volumes, allowing attackers with high local privileges to load unverified content during the boot process. Moreover, a critical pre-authentication server-side request forgery (SSRF) flaw, CVE-2026-102255, in SonicWall's SMA1000 remote-access appliances (models 6210, 7210, and 8200v running firmware versions 12.4.3-03526 and 12.5.0-02952 or earlier) is under active exploitation just days after a patch was released, highlighting a recurring pattern of rapid exploitation of internet-facing VPN gateways.

In the realm of hardware hacking and IoT security, a significant supply chain attack impacted Ledger hardware wallets, with attackers physically implanting hardware into devices sold through a Southeast Asian reseller, CryptoBilis, resulting in approximately $86 million in drained funds. This incident underscores the increasing threat of physical tampering within the supply chain. RF hacking continues to evolve, with emerging signal-based threats for 2026 including RF spoofing against autonomous systems, GNSS manipulation, Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) relay expansion, and covert RF channels in IoT fleets. New tools like the HackRF Pro, a successor to the HackRF One, are expanding capabilities for RF analysis and transmission across a broad spectrum from 1 MHz to 6 GHz. Research into side-channel attacks is also advancing, with studies exploring power LED-based techniques for key extraction and eavesdropping.
